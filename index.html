1. Problem Statement & Research Questions

This interactive visualization tool explores critical questions about global carbon emissions:

  • How have CO₂ emission trends evolved across major nations from 2000 to 2019?
  • Which countries show the fastest emission growth, and which are declining?
  • What is the magnitude of emission gaps between developed and developing nations?
  • What critical turning points occurred during this two-decade period?

Through interactive exploration, users can uncover patterns in carbon emissions across four representative countries—China, USA, India, and Germany—revealing the impact of industrialization, economic development, and environmental policies.

2. Design Rationale

Visualization Choices

Primary Chart (Line + Scatter): Optimal for temporal trends with precise data points. Alternatives like area charts would cause occlusion, while bar charts would be too cluttered with 20 years of data.

Bar Chart: Ideal for cross-country comparison within a single year, updating dynamically on hover.

Trend Analysis (Growth Rate): Displays year-over-year percentage changes, revealing acceleration patterns that absolute values obscure.

Comparison View (Radial): Innovative circular design that is visually engaging and space-efficient compared to traditional bar charts.

Interaction Technologies

Feature Implementation Purpose

Key Design Principles

  • Focus: Four representative countries that tell a compelling story
  • Multi-View Coordination: Four charts update in perfect synchronization
  • Progressive Disclosure: Clean default view with details on interaction
  • Visual Hierarchy: Strategic use of color, size, and opacity

3. Data & Technology

Data Source: CO₂ Emissions Data (2000-2019) for China, USA, India, and Germany from data.csv

Technology Stack:

  • D3.js v7 - Core visualization library
  • Native HTML/CSS/JavaScript - No additional frameworks

Inspiration: NameVoyager (dynamic query), Zipdecode (interactive exploration), GitHub Dark Mode (theme design)
